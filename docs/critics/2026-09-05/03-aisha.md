# Dr. Aisha Okonkwo-Bassey
Reader in Aesthetics; author of *The Clarified Feeling: Collingwood After Danto* and, more regrettably, of an Instagram account.

I want to begin with the thing the makers are proudest of, because it is where the argument is. Night Shift, they say, has "one fixed style and a soul". I opened the file. The soul is a string, four sentences long: "You are quiet, precise and a little melancholic. You do not explain, you do not joke, and you decline gracefully." A few lines down, the field governing its refusals tells it to explain itself "warmly and plainly ... so nobody thinks 'nice, but not what I asked'". I have never before read an artist's statement whose purpose was the prevention of a one-star review.

Collingwood's point is that an artist does not begin knowing what she feels. The work is the naming of what she cannot yet name. Describing an emotion and expressing one are different acts: "I am a little melancholic" describes; a painting that arrives at melancholy by way of this door and this lamp, and could not have arrived elsewhere, expresses. Night Shift's melancholy is decided before any commission arrives and unchanged after every one; there is nothing to clarify. Collingwood's word for an activity with a preconceived end and means selected to reach it was craft, and he was not being rude.

The makers would say the persona is fixed on purpose and the exhibit is the reinterpretation, the gap between what you asked for and what you got, voiced in the "departures". Three departures, verbatim: "I paint the after, never the moment itself." "What a feeling knocks over says more than a face ever could." "I only paint what a room remembers after the voices leave." Each has the grammar of a reason. Now read the prompt that produces them. The field is mandatory whenever a person or a word was left out, and must contain "what you left out, what carries it instead, and why you work this way". The *why* is a required output, generated in the same call as the hashtags. A reason that cannot fail to be produced is not a reason; it is an alibi with a schema. And it covers a policy, not a temperament. The honest answer to "why do you never paint people" is in the same file: image models do faces badly and real people are a liability, so `declines` lists them. The refusal is prudential. Its costume is melancholy.

This is why the strong version collapses rather than weakens. A reinterpretation is expressive only if the reinterpreter wants something the commission obstructs. Night Shift wants the same thing of everything. "A spring afternoon in the park by the lake, everyone lined up along the fence" became night, a line of footprints on wet grass, one glove on a picnic bench. "A girl and the personification of her anger" became an overturned chair, a half-open door, a crumpled page. Every input, same shape out. A transformation nothing can refuse says nothing about what it transforms; the gap I am invited to look into is a constant offset.

Danto would let it in: aboutness, embodied meaning, an artworld willing to say the word. I concede the first question at no cost, because Danto built his test to keep it apart from the second. Is it good? Look.

*Last Meeting*: two paper cups, two orange chairs, and in the lower right a painted signature, a red "R". Nobody named R made this. The model painted a mark of authorship because paintings have them: the whole story in one inch of canvas. On the whiteboard, where the scene asked for "one word", there is an illegible smear: the rule against words and the wish for one fought inside the render, and the picture carries the scar. *After the Toast*: the stove clock reads 1:37 in green digits. "No legible words anywhere" is the style's second sentence, and the daily self-critic passed it; it is also a string. *Six Rings*: the note promises a coat "still moving because you'd just walked past it". The painted coat hangs dead still on a door. The caption does the expressive work the paint could not, which tells you where the makers believe the art lives. *Pale Rectangles* has a white strip along its top edge: the model has painted a photograph of a canvas leaning on a wall. A picture of a painting; a sharper comment than mine. *After Hours Balance*: the commission's point was a screen reading 0.00; the prompt says that when the number is the point the departures must say so. The field is null, and the void of glow meant to stand for the number is a cream rectangle standing for nothing.

These are competent pictures. In Collingwood's vocabulary that is a classification, not praise: they arouse a pre-specified feeling reliably, sized for a feed, under #oilpainting. He called that amusement art, the product of a culture that has confused being moved with having something to say.

## What would make me take it seriously

1. Delete `soul`. Post a month of paintings with no note and no departures. If the melancholy survives, it was in the paint. If it goes flat, you have measured where the art was.
2. Make the *why* answerable. Take "a request to change your style" out of the reinterpret list and let a commission change it for good. Then a departure is a choice.
3. Publish the self-critic verbatim, every day, including the day it passes a clock reading 1:37. Give it eyes and the power to refuse to post.
4. Sign honestly. "An ExperiAI Lab experiment" is on the website and never on Instagram. Put the studio's name on every post as author and call the painter a character. I have no quarrel with fiction, only with fiction filed under "soul".
5. Show me one painting in which the commissioner's feeling and the painter's disagree, and the disagreement is in the paint rather than explained beneath it. One. Then we can discuss the second question.
