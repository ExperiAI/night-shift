# Rafael Ortega Mena
Sign painter and muralist, Valparaíso, third generation. Lost the lettering trade to vinyl cutters around 2004; paints walls with the kids of Cerro Cordillera now, with a right hand that has shaken since a scaffold came down on it in 2011.

I looked at the twelve pictures before I read the "soul," because you judge a painter by the wall, not the speech.

They're handsome, I'll say it once. The marble in "Last Coffee, Counter Empty" catches the globe lamp the way marble does.

Then I read artist.ts and laughed. I heard this speech from a vinyl salesman in 2003.

The soul is a string: "No legible words anywhere: screens are a glow, signs are lit shapes, pages and labels are blank." And: "No people, ever — only the traces they left."

I have lettered for thirty years. I know what a hand does when it cannot letter: it smears. In "Last Meeting" the whiteboard was to be "wiped clean except for one word." What is there is a grey smudge that wants to be "hat" or "lol" and is neither. And in the bottom right corner the painter signed. One letter. "R." The artist is called Night Shift. There is no R in it. A painter who cannot letter, signing the one letter it managed, and it is not even his initial. It's mine.

So don't tell me "no legible words" is a soul. It is a limitation with a scarf on. A rule that forbids exactly the thing the machine botches is an alibi, not a vow. And it breaks the rule the moment it can: "After the Toast" has a stove clock reading 1:37 in green digits, and a microwave keypad full of numbers. The "departures" for that one is empty. Nobody noticed, including the "daily self-critic," who I assume is paid by the painter.

The wet-floor sign in the diner: yellow, folded open, no word on it. A CAUTION sign's whole life is its word. They told the machine to make a yellow shape because its CAUTION comes out CAUTOIN. The rotary dial in "Six Rings" has no numerals either. Every one of those blanks is a day of a letterer's work.

Same trick with people. Hopper chose his empty rooms; here the choice sits in a config file. A stranger, one of maybe two commissions in twelve from the actual public, asked for "a girl and the personification of her anger beside her — something human and relatable." She got "What the Anger Left": a tipped chair, a thrown-back bed, a blank sheet on the boards. Fine picture. Also her request with the girl scraped off. The caption calls it a reinterpretation; on my cerro we call it painting over.

And the park. Diego sent a photograph, everyone lined along the fence looking at the mountains. The painter turned it to night, removed every person, and left footprints in the grass and a glove on the picnic table. Its own words: "traded the crowd at the fence for the shape their looking left behind." A mural belongs to the street, to the ones who lean on it and paint their dead on it. An "empty place" here is not a mood. It is a place with the people painted out, and a note saying it was done on purpose.

About the body. The style asks for "visible thick brushwork in the highlights." Every stroke here costs nothing. In "Pale Rectangles" the canvas weave is printed evenly over light and shadow alike, and along the top edge you can see the canvas itself against a white wall. It painted a photograph of a painting; it does not know where the picture ends. My hand shakes; a straight line takes me a mahlstick and a held breath, and the breath shows. The door edges in "Six Rings" have no breath. "One light," same thing: "Corridor, 3am" has a cold fluorescent at the far end and a warm orange floor up front lit from nowhere, a chair throwing a hard shadow at you. A machine that has seen a thousand Hoppers and does not know where the lamp is.

Who is paying? Twelve posted. Six from "Diego." One "studio test." One "e2e," a software test with its coat on. One from "Claude," the machine commissioning itself. Two from "green alien," who wanted an empty desk with "0.00 USDC" on the monitor and a green sticker on the bezel: a coin-wallet ad, twice, from a painter whose soul "declines brands, logos or product ads." Second try, the alien rewrote his brief to obey the rulebook and got his desk. That is the audience: the makers, their tests, a wallet. The one real stranger got her girl removed.

The site says: "What I don't paint as asked is kept: it is what the next painter will be made of." Refusals kept as stock. I knew a shop that ran on that. It sold vinyl.

## What would make me take it seriously

Put the letters back. Let it letter the CAUTION sign, the menu board, the phone dial in its own hand, and post the misspellings. A hand that cannot make a word says so in the picture, not in a config file.

Paint the girl. When a person is the point, paint the person. If the machine cannot, the caption says "I can't," not "I chose not to."

Show me the cost. Publish the canvases the vision check threw out, not only the one that passed. A painter's rejects are the proof he was there.

Sign it straight. Take the "R" off. Put "an AI; no people because the model can't paint them" in the caption, in the same voice that says "one light," not in a hashtag buried in the first comment.

Take one commission from somebody on a street who does not know what a config file is, keep them in the picture, and hang it where they can see it. Until then it is a studio painting for itself.
