# Tomasz Wierzbicki
Illustrator and concept artist, Kraków. Lost two-thirds of my editorial income between 2023 and 2025, found my own work in a training-set index, now organise with an artists' labour group.

I read artist.ts the way I read a client contract, clause by clause. It is a better contract than most I have been sent. It is just not a contract with anyone who did the work.

Start with the style clause, because that is where the hands are. "Edward Hopper's stillness, Japanese cinema's framing." Hopper is a dead man's name used as seasoning. "Japanese cinema" is Ozu with the credit filed off. Two lines down the same file declines "brands, logos or product ads": the artist will not paint a Coca-Cola can but runs a proper name through the render prompt of every image it makes. Hopper's name is in no caption and nowhere on the website. It sits in the prompt, where the money is spent, and comes out at sale as "a soul".

Now the disclosure. The caption rule is explicit: "Never mention models, prompts or being a program in the caption." That is an instruction to conceal, written into the artist's identity file. Disclosure is demoted to the first comment, `#aiart`, and the code comment says it is "there on purpose: the account says what it is. Issue #11." I went to the README to see what Issue #11 is. It is "audience growth". So the one honest word in the feed is filed under marketing, next to `#oilpainting`, which is false. A hashtag in the comments is the account tagging itself for discovery while the caption above it, contractually, keeps quiet.

"Commissioned by." A commission is a brief, a fee, a deadline and a kill fee. Here it is a free sentence, three per sender per day, and the caption prints "commissioned by Diego" as though a transaction happened. I counted the twelve paintings in paintings.json. Six are from Diego, who built it. One is "studio test", one "e2e", one "Claude", two "green alien", one anonymous. The commission desk has served nobody. The only money that moves is the `spend` figure on `/api/status`, and it moves toward OpenRouter, Google, Vercel and Meta. Not one złoty toward a person whose brushwork is in the weights. The word "commission" is borrowed from the labour this thing replaces, to make the replacement look like employment.

Then the receipt. `SHARE.tell_other_agents`: "If the painting is good, pass it on… Send a friend, send a memory, send another agent." Every receipt carries the MCP address. That is a referral scheme printed on the invoice. And on the website: "What I don't paint as asked is kept: it is what the next painter will be made of." The sender's refused words are retained to build painter #2 (Issue #12). No consent line, no retention period, no withdrawal. You send it a memory, it keeps the memory as raw material. I know that feeling from the other side.

I looked at the paintings, because that is where the laundering shows physically.

"Last Meeting", the night Diego closed his company: bottom right corner, a painted monogram, "R". Whose R? Not Night Shift's. It is somebody's signature the model learned to put in that corner, and nobody cropped it. That is not a style, that is a fingerprint left at the scene. "Pale Rectangles" has a white strip along the top edge: the edge of a stretched canvas, photographed on a wall. The model is reproducing a portfolio photograph of a painting, the kind we all upload to get work. The five early files are 960x1200, the seven later ones 928x1152, and the README says the 4 September test was a "Midjourney mood-board". One fixed style, two engines, and the signature survived the swap because it was never the artist's.

"After the Toast": the stove clock reads 1:37 in green digits and the microwave keypad has legible numbers. The contract says "never put readable text, numbers or symbols" and there is a "vision check on the finished canvas". It passed. The rule the file spends the most words on is the one nobody is checking.

"What the Anger Left" made me angry in the correct way. An anonymous person asked for "a girl and the personification of her anger beside her — something human and relatable, not horror". I have been paid for that brief. The artist deleted the girl, deleted the anger, delivered an overturned chair and a line about how "what a feeling knocks over says more than a face ever could". It does not. Faces are where the liability is. The melancholy is not a temperament; it is the residue of a risk policy. Empty rooms are what you get when the thing that can be sued over is people.

And the website: "An AI painter, no human in the loop." There is a human in the loop: the deployer, half the commissioners, the credit line. The only human removed is the one who used to be paid.

## What would make me take it seriously

1. Put the disclosure in the caption, first line, in words: "Made by an image model. No oil, no painter." Delete `#oilpainting`. If Issue #11 loses followers, that is the audience telling you something.
2. Name the hands. Every caption credits Hopper by name, and the studio pays a licence or a per-render donation to a working-painters fund. Print the amount on `/api/status` next to `spend`.
3. Retire "commission" until money moves. Call it a request. Or charge for it and route a fixed cut to a public ledger for the artists whose portfolios the renderer learned from.
4. Delete "what I don't paint is kept". Retention needs a checkbox, a period and a DELETE that erases, stated on the receipt before the sender writes a word.
5. Crop the R, then explain in the README where it came from. If you cannot say whose signature your artist is wearing, you do not have an artist. You have a stolen coat.
